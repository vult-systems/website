This past spring I had the opportunity to travel to Mexico City to visit **Centro de Integración y Wdesarrollo (CIW)**, our sister university, for a two-day visit built around teaching, conversation, and connection.

The trip was a long time coming.

We've been building out a relationship with CIW over the past year, and this visit was a chance to make it real — to put faces to names, walk through their facilities, and see firsthand what their students and faculty are working on.

## The Sessions

The visit centered around two back-to-back teaching blocks.

The first was a **2-hour masterclass** — a focused presentation on character creation workflows, covering the pipeline from concept to final asset. The room was full, the students were engaged, and the conversation that came out of it was genuinely great. There were sharp questions, real curiosity about industry workflows, and a hunger to push their work further.

The second block was a **2-hour studio workshop** — more hands-on, more intimate. We worked through specific problems together, I gave feedback on student work in progress, and it turned into one of those sessions that feels less like a class and more like a conversation between artists.

That's always the goal.

## The People and the Place

Beyond the sessions, what made the trip stand out was the time spent with the CIW faculty and staff. Meeting the team in person changes things. You get a real sense of their values, their vision for the program, and the challenges they're navigating — some of which aren't that different from ours.

We toured their facilities, talked through curriculum structure, and had a lot of candid conversations about where animation and game art education is heading. There's genuine alignment between what they're building and what we're working toward — that's not something you can assume going in, so finding it was encouraging.

Mexico City itself was worth the trip on its own terms. Dense, layered, full of visual noise in the best way. The kind of city that reminds you why so many artists end up in it.

## What's Next

This visit wasn't a one-off. The intent on both sides is to keep building — student exchanges, shared curriculum resources, collaborative opportunities for faculty. The foundation is there.

More to come.
