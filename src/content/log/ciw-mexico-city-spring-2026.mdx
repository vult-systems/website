Every year, faculty from our university travel to our sister schools as part of a broader effort to build relationships, strengthen programs, and stay aligned across campuses. Last year, I had the opportunity to visit our sister campus in [Bajío](https://uiwbajio.mx/estudiar-animacion-3d-y-diseno-de-videojuegos-en-uiw-bajio/). This year, toward the end of the Spring 2026 semester, I found myself in Mexico City visiting **[Centro Universitario Incarnate Word (CIW)](https://www.ciw.edu.mx/)**.

The trip was multifaceted.

Part teaching. Part collaboration. Part relationship building. Part making sure we are all heading in the same direction as programs and industry continue evolving.

It was productive to say the least. Meeting faculty in person, spending time with students, seeing the resources and facilities firsthand, and identifying where programs align, where they differ, and where we can continue strengthening the bridge between campuses.

## Boots On The Ground

The visit centered around two teaching blocks.

The first was a 2-hour masterclass, focused on my background, character creation with a production mindset, and the realities of building work for games and film. I always enjoy these kinds of sessions because they become less about presenting information and more about opening conversations around process, problem solving, worries, doubts, and the weird balancing act between art, technical execution, and production realities.

The second block shifted into a 2-hour studio workshop, critiques, and feedback session were to follow.

Honestly, the studio hands-on workshops are unlike anything else. They're what keep me going. 

There is something really rewarding about sitting down with students, seeing where they are at, hearing what they are struggling with, and helping problem solve in real time. Whether it is anatomy, design decisions, workflow issues, or simply confidence in their work, workshop sessions always feel like the place where I can genuinely help the students beat their game. Whatever that means to them. I'm there for it all and to help as much as I possibly can. 

## The Birds Eye View

Outside of teaching, one of the biggest goals of the trip was simply getting to know the team.

Meeting faculty face-to-face changes things. Conversations become more candid. You gain a better understanding of what each campus is navigating, what resources exist, what limitations are present, and how programs are evolving in response.

I was able to tour their facilities, talked curriculum, explored resources, and had a lot of conversations about how we can continue aligning programs while still respecting the needs and realities of each campus.

And of course, there was also Mexico City itself.

Getting to explore the city, experience the culture, and spend time outside the university setting was equally as valuable. Trips like this remind me how important context is. Programs do not exist in a vacuum. Culture, environment, resources, and local perspectives all shape the kinds of artists and students we are helping develop.

## What's To Come

The trip was short, but very meaningful.

Relationships were made and also reinforced. Missing links became a little clearer. New ideas surfaced. And perhaps most importantly, the bridge between campuses felt just a bit smaller.

I left with a couple initiatives planned and in the coming year I'm thrilled to see where it goes and what future opportunities emerge for students, faculty, and collaboration across campuses. Should be fun!
